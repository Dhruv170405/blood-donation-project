Clinical Reality

Why Blood is Essential

Where does your donated blood go? Hospitals rely on scheduled donations every day for these life-saving procedures.

🚗

Emergency Trauma

Accident and trauma victims suffering acute hemorrhage often need 30 to 50 blood units within minutes to prevent shock.

Need: Up to 50 Units
🏥

Major Surgeries

Open-heart operations, joint replacements, and organ transplants require multiple units of packed red blood cells on hand.

Need: 2 to 10+ Units
🩺

Cancer Support

Chemotherapy destroys both cancer cells and healthy bone marrow. Regular platelet transfusions keep cancer patients stable.

Need: Weekly Transfusions
👶

Maternal & Neonatal

Postpartum hemorrhage in new mothers and premature babies in NICU units rely on specialized, screened micro-donations.

Need: Immediate Access

Safety First

Eligibility & What to Expect

Donating blood is simple, quick, and safe. Here are the basic qualifications and the standard 4-step process.

1

Registration & ID

Present a photo ID and complete a confidential health history questionnaire.

2

Mini-Screening

Staff check temperature, pulse, blood pressure, and a quick fingerstick hemoglobin test.

3

10-Min Donation

Relax comfortably while approximately 1 pint of blood is collected into a sterile bag.

4

Rest & Refresh

Enjoy free juices and snacks for 15 minutes before heading back to your routine.

Standard Donor Criteria

  • Age: 18 – 65 years (16–17 with parental consent in some regions).
  • Weight: At least 50 kg (110 lbs) for a standard whole blood donation.
  • Hemoglobin: Minimum 12.5 g/dL (tested free of cost at the drive).
  • Time Interval: At least 56 days (8 weeks) between whole blood donations.
  • Wellness: Feeling healthy, well-hydrated, with no cold, flu, or fever.
